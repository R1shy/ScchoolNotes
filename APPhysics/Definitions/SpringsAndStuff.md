## Spring Constant
$F_s=-k\times\Delta x$
$k$ is the spring constant
$x$ is the distance stretched / compressed from equilibrium
$F$ is the force that creates the stretch / compression
the spring constant represents how stiff a spring is
springs that are harder to  pull / push have a higher spring constant
spring constant is represented by $k$


### Way to find number 1
hang a spring
attach different masses
note down the length of the spring when masses are applied
$k=\frac{F}{x}$
$F$ is the weight of the mass
x is the length of the spring
$k$ is the spring constant
if you get different values (EX: $0.20$ and $0.21$) then your answer would be $k=0.20\pm0.01$

### Way to find number 2
using a spring scale
attach one end to a spring scale
pull on the other end with your finger
put the spring up against a ruler to measure the length
graph $F$ as a function of $x$ the equation for which is $F=kx$
$k$ is the slope of the line

## Way to find number 3
use a digital force probe for an exact amount of force
do it twice and average you $k=\frac{F}{x}$ EX: $k=20N\pm1$

### Shortcuts
when mass is hung from a spring, the force applied to the spring ($F$) is equal to the weight of the mass
