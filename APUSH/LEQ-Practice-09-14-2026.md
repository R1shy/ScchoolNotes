All the colonies wanted to leave England, but that is where the similarities end. The south focused heavily on cash crops and farming which is what their climate was good at growing, the north became much more industrial especially with the coal boom in the Appalachian mountains. These differences of farming in the south which is heavily benefited by unpaid and forced labor and religious priorities in the north allowed them to slowly create a more stable economy that was not entirely reliant on slave labor like in the south.

Tobacco was the biggest cash crop. People often think that from when the colonies were founded pre revolution the best cash crop that all the southern colonies were growing was cotton, but until the invention of the cotton gin in 1793 tobacco was much easier and more profitable to grow, of course the more tobacco plantations were growing the more that needed to be harvested but hiring workers to harvest at the scale plantations were growing too would require more money than plantation owners were willing to spend which led them to rely on slavery, but as the southern economy became more and more reliant on slavery they sentenced themself to death if slavery was ever abolished which in part lead to the Civil War and the formation of the confederacy.

The north was very religious and very industrialist. The north never turned to slavery because they cared less about making lots of money like the south and more about escaping religious persecution. Massachusetts was founded on Puritan ideals and the founders came on the Mayflower to found a place where they could be proudly puritan, and could stay far away from the King on the other side of the Atlantic. Not all northern colonies were focused on a single religious state though, Rhode Island had a law of acceptance which mandated religious freedom.
