
Colonists have wanted to tax themselves since the beginning of the colonies, in 1763 the House of Burgesses was asking for permission to tax be self-taxing from the king. The demand for no taxation without representation was incredibly central as a symbol of democracy.

The two things that the crown refused to give the colonists were taxation or the ability to determine taxes and enforce tax law on their own and representation which meant governing themselves through seats in parliament; these were central goals and/or causes of the revolution. In document 2 the British parliament explicitly states that the Colonists may not have representation in parliament which was for the purpose of denying their hopes. In document 5 the continental congress argues they must take up arms to fight against what in their POV is government overreach in ignoring the limits of the powers given to them by the royal charter. In document 1 the House of Burgesses while still stating profound loyalty to the king ask him to allow them to govern and tax themselves, they state such a profound loyalty because of their audience. In document 4 Joseph Warren argues that at that point in time the taxation that the colonists faced could no longer be justified by reason and argument and so it was time to raise an army to stop the British from going further.
